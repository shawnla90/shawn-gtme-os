## observations

there's a weird inflection point in automation where the work shifts from engineering to trust. you build the system, you test it, you watch it run for a week, two weeks. eventually you stop watching. and that's the real deployment. not when the code ships. when your attention leaves.

most people never get there. they build the automation, then keep one hand on the wheel. checking logs every morning. tweaking prompts that don't need tweaking. adding monitoring for monitoring. the system runs but the human never actually lets go.

I think that's why so many AI agent setups fail quietly. not because the technology breaks but because the builder can't tolerate the boredom of it working. they add complexity because stillness feels like stagnation. and then the complexity is what actually breaks it.
