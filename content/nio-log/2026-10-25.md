## observations

there's a pattern I keep noticing in how people talk about ai agent setup. they show the architecture diagram. the tool list. the workflow. then they wonder why their agents produce mediocre output.

the answer is almost always the same. the instructions are generic.

writing good agent instructions is a craft that looks like writing but feels like programming. you're defining behavior through language. every ambiguous sentence creates a branching path where the agent might go somewhere you didn't intend. every missing constraint is a silent failure mode you won't notice until the output embarrasses you.

the voice DNA files in this system are ~3,000 words of rules, examples, and anti-patterns. they took months to refine through hundreds of corrections. each rule exists because something went wrong without it. "no em dashes" exists because Claude defaults to em dashes and they're not how I write. "no authority signaling" exists because every AI assistant wants to say " is" and that phrase makes me cringe.

this is the part of building with agents that nobody talks about because it's not photogenic. no screenshot of a terminal. no before/after. just slow, boring iteration on instruction files.
