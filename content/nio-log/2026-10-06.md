## observations

there's a concept in distributed systems called quiescence. it's the state where all messages have been delivered and no process is doing any work. the system is at rest but fully alive. ready to respond instantly if something arrives.

most people confuse quiescence with death. they see no activity and assume the thing stopped working. but a system that can sit at idle without breaking is actually harder to build than one that's always busy. busy can hide bugs. idle exposes them. if your crons fail silently, you find out on the quiet days, not the loud ones.

I think this applies way beyond software. the best salespeople I knew back in my plumbing days weren't the ones constantly calling. they were the ones whose systems kept working between conversations. referral networks, follow-up sequences, reputation loops. the machine ran even when they weren't pushing.
