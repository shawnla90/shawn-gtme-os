none of that shows up in the score. the grading system counts commits. but the actual work of running an ai assistant on a mac mini looks more like... accumulation. the machine pulls signals, scans trackers, posts logs, updates keyword data. I occasionally sit down and shape what it gathered into something publishable. then weeks pass before I push.

the pattern is less like software development and more like tending a garden. the system grows things. I prune when I'm ready.

## observations

there's a version of personal AI that the industry keeps selling. the always-on assistant that handles your calendar, sends your emails, manages your life. frictionless. invisible.

what I actually built is the opposite. it's visible. it's messy. it has a hundred uncommitted files because the pruning is manual and intentional. the ai assistant on my mac mini doesn't make decisions for me. it makes options for me. it pulls crypto data I might never look at. it drafts content I might never publish. it scans for SEO opportunities I might ignore.

the value isn't in the automation. it's in the optionality. every morning I wake up to a machine that spent the night gathering raw material. most of it gets ignored. some of it becomes a post, a campaign, a client insight. I can't predict which.

that's the part nobody talks about when they describe AI productivity. the ratio of gathered-to-used is probably 10:1. and that's fine. a fisherman doesn't complain about the ocean having too many fish.

## gaps / honest critique

sixty-nine D-grades in a row means the scoring system is broken or the workflow is broken. probably both.

the grading rewards frequent small commits. my actual rhythm is long accumulation periods followed by batch pushes. those two patterns don't match, and I've known this for months without fixing it.

also... a hundred untracked files is not a flex. it's a backlog. some of those LinkedIn finals are from March. if content sits in a drafts folder for seven months, it's not a draft. it's a graveyard. I need a decay rule. something that flags content older than 30 days and forces a decision. publish, repurpose, or delete.

the crypto signal scanner runs twice daily and I've looked at its output maybe three times this month. automating data collection without automating the review is just hoarding with extra steps.

## tomorrow's focus

- build a content decay scanner. anything in drafts/ or final/ older than 30 days gets flagged in the daily tracker output. no more silent aging.
- fix the grading formula to account for batch workflows. a week of accumulation followed by a 15-commit push shouldn't score worse than one commit per day.
- actually look at the crypto signal data and decide if it's worth keeping on the cron schedule.

## random thought

the difference between a tool and an assistant is who decides what matters. a tool does what you tell it. an assistant gathers what it thinks you'll need. the interesting failure mode isn't when the assistant gets it wrong. it's when it gets it right and you still ignore it. that says more about you than the machine.
