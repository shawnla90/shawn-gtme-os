so here's what I want to talk about instead. the question that eats more time than any actual building: picking your ai agent automation tools.

I watch people spend weeks on this. they make comparison spreadsheets. they join Discord servers for tools they haven't used yet. they read seventeen blog posts titled "the best AI agent framework in 2026" and come away more confused than when they started.

the dirty secret is that the tools are the least interesting decision you'll make.

I run about a dozen automated agents across this system. cron jobs, content pipelines, market scrapers, a CRM that updates itself. the tools involved are... not exciting. SQLite. Python scripts. a few API calls. launchd plists that fire at specific times. Claude for the parts that need reasoning.

nobody would put that stack on a comparison chart. but it's been running for months without babysitting.

here's what actually matters when you're choosing ai agent automation tools: not the tool's feature list, but how it connects to the thing before it and the thing after it. the integration layer. the wiring.

a fancy agent framework that can't read your database is useless. a simple Python script that reads your database and writes to your CRM is infrastructure.

I used to be a plumber. literal pipes, not data pipes. and the thing about plumbing is nobody cares what brand your fittings are. they care that water comes out when they turn the handle. the fitting just has to connect to what's upstream and what's downstream. that's it.

same principle applies here. your agent tool just needs to read from where data lives and write to where data needs to go. everything else is marketing.

## observations

there's a pattern I keep noticing in how people approach automation versus how it actually works in production.

the fantasy version: you pick the perfect tool, configure it once, and it handles everything forever.

the reality version: you pick a decent tool, wire it to your specific data, fix the three edge cases that break in the first week, and then it runs. the tool selection was maybe 5% of the total effort. the wiring and edge case handling was the other 95%.

this is why the D-grade streak is actually instructive. these crons aren't running because I picked brilliant tools. they're running because I spent the time wiring them correctly to my specific data sources and outputs. the tools themselves are boring. the connections are what took the work.
