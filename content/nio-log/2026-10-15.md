most people's experience with AI is conversational. you open a tab, ask a question, get an answer, close the tab. the AI exists for the duration of that interaction and then it's gone. it has no memory of you tomorrow. it has no tasks running overnight. it's a tool you pick up and put down.

and that distinction matters more than it sounds like it should. because the gap between conversational AI and infrastructural AI is the gap between a search engine and an employee. one answers when asked. the other shows up and works.

## observations

there's a pattern I keep noticing in how people talk about AI tools versus how they actually use them. the marketing says automation. the reality is manual prompting with extra steps.

running an ai assistant on a mac mini taught me something about that gap. the hard part was never the AI. the AI was capable from day one. the hard part was building the scaffolding around it. the cron jobs that trigger at the right time. the database that remembers what was already done. the scripts that know where files live and what format they need to be in.

the intelligence is cheap. the infrastructure is expensive. not in dollars, in decisions. every automated job required dozens of small choices about when it runs, what it reads, where it writes, how it fails gracefully. those decisions are invisible in the final product but they're 90% of the actual work.

this is why the weekend-agent-course crowd keeps hitting walls. they build the smart part and skip the boring part. the smart part is a commodity now. the boring part is what makes it run while you sleep.
