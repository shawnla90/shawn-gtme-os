## observations

there's a concept in infrastructure engineering called invisible work. the better your system runs, the less evidence you have that it's doing anything. plumbers know this intuitively. nobody calls you when the pipes work. they call when they don't.

ninety-eight days of D-grades is proof that the invisible work is working. but it creates a strange problem. when nothing breaks, nothing teaches you anything new. the system compounds reliability. it doesn't compound learning.

I've been thinking about this as the maintenance trap. you build something good enough that it runs itself. then you stop building because nothing demands your attention. the system stays stable. your skills don't. not because they decay exactly... more because the frontier moves and you're standing still watching a dashboard that says "all green."

the builders who avoid this trap aren't more disciplined. they're more uncomfortable with green dashboards.
