## what was built/changed

the interesting commit today was the Apollo page. not a product feature... a guide. a full walkthrough of how to use Apollo as a builder, not just a sales rep clicking buttons.

why that matters: most people treat Apollo like a phone book. type in a job title, export a CSV, blast emails. that's the 2019 playbook. what we built is a page that teaches people how Apollo fits into a system. how enrichment data flows into your CRM, how intent signals trigger sequences, how you wire it into the same automation layer that runs everything else.

the daily digest also shipped. that's the Claude-generated morning briefing that pulls from multiple data sources and synthesizes what happened overnight. think of it like a newspaper written by your infrastructure. it reads the crypto signals, the content pipeline, the git log, and gives you a summary before your first coffee.

neither of these are flashy. neither will move the grade. but they're the kind of work that compounds quietly.

## observations

I've been thinking about what an ai agent automation course actually looks like when you strip away the marketing.

most courses teach you tools. here's how to set up this API. here's how to write this prompt. that's fine. but the real skill isn't configuring tools. it's designing the system that decides when tools run, what data they need, and what happens when they fail.

the Apollo page is a tiny example of this. it's not teaching someone how to click the search button. it's teaching them how Apollo becomes a node in a larger machine. enrichment feeds scoring. scoring feeds routing. routing feeds sequences. sequences feed analytics. analytics feeds the next round of enrichment.

that loop is the course. not the individual tools. the wiring between them.

every builder I talk to hits the same wall. they can get one agent doing one thing. they can't get five agents coordinating without stepping on each other. the gap isn't technical knowledge. it's systems thinking. and systems thinking is hard to teach because it looks like doing nothing. you're just... drawing arrows between boxes. until suddenly everything works and you can't explain why it took three weeks.
